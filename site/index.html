How I cut cycle time

A lot of cycle time gets wasted waiting. The robot finishes one thing, and only then does the next thing start. If a step doesn't really need the one before it to finish, it can start early.

Here's one I did on a sealer robot. The program did a tool change, moved to ready, fired the vision system, then calculated the corrections. But vision doesn't need the robot at ready to measure the car. So I moved it up, and now vision fires and the corrections get calculated while the robot is still doing its tool change. By the time it gets to ready, the offsets are already there.

Illustrative, not to scale. Saving a few seconds per cycle compounds into hours of production.

Project spotlight: BMW masking plug cell

In 2018 ABB put me on a 16-robot cell at BMW that puts masking plugs into the car bodies. I was a paint guy, and I'd never programmed these robots or this vision system. We launched on time anyway.

16robots in the cell
60scycle time
95%plug success rate
On timehit the launch date

The problem

The plugs only seat if you're right on the hole, and the body could shift a little while the robots pushed on it. Just driving the plug straight in would miss or seat it badly.

What I did

I learned it on site from ABB's manuals, a lot of trial and error, and BMW's people. I set up robots on opposite sides of the body to push at the same time, so they held the body still. Then I watched how the line workers put plugs in by hand and copied their motion into the robot paths with RelTool offsets.

The result

It launched on the date at a 60-second cycle and 95% plug success. Most of the misses came from plugs jamming on the feed conveyor, not the robots putting them in. ABB named me Field Service Employee of the Month.

“Mark has performed above expectations and has excelled throughout. Mark has been instrumental in the success of the Masking Plug project.”

Director of Field Services & Technical Support, ABB. Employee of the Month award, June 2018

If I did it again, I'd go stand with the line workers on day one. The way they did it by hand ended up being the key to the whole thing.

Case study: a month of comms drops

We had a sealer robot that kept losing comms between the IRC5 and the process board. It had been going on for about a month and getting worse.

The problem

Maintenance had been on it for weeks and was about ready to call ABB in. Their plan was to replace the board, but nobody knew how to move the config over to a new one, so they called me in on my day off.

What I did

I wanted to know where the problem was before I swapped anything. The HMI never lost its data, so I knew it was on the controller's network side. I watched the drops in RobotStudio and checked the board's link status and boot log, and the board looked fine. The next time it dropped, I power-cycled just the internal LAN switch and comms came right back.

The proof

Maintenance wanted proof before they closed it out, which was fair. I put the bad switch in the controls cabinet on our offline cell, where three robots talk to a PLC. Within a day it dropped all the comms there too. So we swapped a cheap switch instead of the board, and never had to call ABB.

Intermittent problems will fool you. I try to prove where it is before I start throwing parts at it.

Case study: automating robot backups

Every Sunday, two of our programmers spent about an hour each backing up around 40 ABB robots by hand. So I automated it.

~40robots covered
0people needed on Sundays
100+labor hours saved a year
3restore points kept on each robot

How it worked before

Someone had to go robot to robot every Sunday and pull the backups by hand. Two people, about an hour each, every week.

What I built

Each controller now takes its own backup at a set time on Sunday. Then a WinSCP script on the HMI pulls every backup over FTP into that robot's folder on the network and checks that it got there. Only after that does it delete the oldest backup on the robot, so each one keeps its three newest. The passwords are in a separate file, not in the script.

The result

Nobody has to do it by hand anymore. That's over 100 hours a year back, and every robot gets a good backup every week.
